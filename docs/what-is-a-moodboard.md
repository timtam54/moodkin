# What is a Moodboard?

A moodboard is essentially a **visual collaboration tool** used in creative projects to establish shared aesthetic direction before the actual work begins.

## How it works in practice

### The concept

Both the photographer and client gather images that capture the "feel" they're imagining for the project — this might include:

- Lighting styles
- Colour palettes
- Poses
- Locations
- Textures
- Abstract imagery that evokes the right mood

It's less about literal examples and more about communicating intangible qualities like "warm and intimate" or "bold and editorial."

### The collaboration process

Each party uploads images independently, then reviews what the other has contributed. The like/dislike mechanism creates a voting system that surfaces:

- **Where visions align** — both like the same images
- **Where they diverge** — one likes something the other dislikes

### The outcome

By the end of the process, you have a curated set of mutually-approved reference images that guide the actual shoot. This prevents the classic creative misalignment where a client says "natural and candid" but means something completely different to what the photographer interprets.

## Why photographers use them

It's a **communication bridge**. Clients often struggle to articulate what they want verbally, but can easily point at images and say "like this."

It also **protects the photographer** — if the final work aligns with the approved moodboard, there's less room for "this isn't what I expected" disputes.

## So what exactly IS the moodboard?

The moodboard isn't a separate thing you create at the end. **It IS the collection of images itself.** Think of it as a shared pinboard or collage where all the uploaded images live together.

In Moodkin's context, the moodboard is essentially the "project" or "workspace" that the photographer creates and invites their client to. Everything happens within that moodboard — both parties upload to it, browse it, and react to images within it.

## The workflow

1. **Photographer creates a new moodboard** (gives it a name, links it to a project or shoot date)
2. **Photographer invites the client** (via email link)
3. **Both upload images** to that shared moodboard
4. **Both browse and like/dislike images**
5. **The moodboard gradually reveals their shared vision** through the voting

The moodboard is just the container for the whole collaboration. Some people use physical corkboards with printed photos and magazine cutouts pinned to them — Moodkin is the digital equivalent of that board.

## AI-Powered Moodboard Generation

When you click "Create Moodboard", the system:

1. Collects all images from the project
2. Scores each image based on reactions (+1 for likes, -2 for flags)
3. Filters out heavily flagged images
4. Sorts by score (best images first)
5. Sends top images to OpenAI GPT-4 Vision to generate an evocative title and description
6. Saves the curated collection as an exportable PDF

This distills the collaboration into a polished deliverable that captures the shared creative vision.
